# Phrases

This page lists how words stand together in a phrase: who acts, what the
action is done to, and in which order the words come.

## The shape of a phrase

A phrase is built around a verb. The verb carries its subject as a prefix,
so a phrase may be one word: `ozu` is *I love*.

The other words say who takes part. A case on each word says what it does.

| Case | Gloss | It marks | Its place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `m` | `ACC` | what the action is done to | a suffix, always there |
| `ni` | `DAT` | the one that the action is for | a prefix |
| `fu` | `ERG` | the one who acts | a prefix, and only for stress or to be clear |
| `wu` | `GEN` | what is owned, before its owner | a suffix |

The one who acts most often has no case, and the object always has one.
Since the cases say what each word does, the order of the words is free.

An example has three lines: the words cut into their morphemes, the gloss
of each word, and the translation.

    John    a-guwu      tame-m      ni-Jane
    John    3SG-give    ball-ACC    DAT-Jane
    'John gives the ball to Jane.'
