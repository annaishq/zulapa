# Glossing

A gloss says what each morpheme of a word means. This site writes its
glosses by the [Leipzig Glossing Rules](https://www.eva.mpg.de/lingua/resources/glossing-rules.php),
the convention that linguists share. This page lists the rules that the
glosses of this site follow, and every label they use.

## The rules of a gloss

An example has three lines: the words cut into their morphemes, the gloss,
and the translation.

    es-o-lu-zu
    PASS-1-CONT-love
    'I am being loved.'

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| A hyphen parts two morphemes, in the word and in the gloss. Both have as many hyphens. | `o-zu`, `1-love` |
| A period parts two meanings of one morpheme. | `ti`, `2SG.POSS` |
| A label in capitals is a grammatical category. It is listed in the table below. | `PST`, `ACC` |
| A person and its number stand together, with no period. | `2SG`, `3PL` |
| A meaning in lower case is a translation. It is one word, or words joined by periods. | `listen`, `fall.asleep` |
| A root with several senses holds them all at once. Its gloss gives them all, joined by periods. | `gu`, `darkness.empathy.life` |
| A root is glossed by the reading that its word gives it. | `agu` is `ear` in a noun and `listen` in a verb |
| A joining sound is a segment of its own, glossed `EP`. | `o-x-agu`, `1-EP-listen` |

The build checks each gloss of a rule against these: the count of its
parts, the place of each `EP`, and that every label is listed.

## The labels

A label comes from one of three places. *Leipzig* is the list of the
Leipzig Glossing Rules. *Common* is a label in wide use that this list does
not have. *Zulapa* is a category of the language, named here.
