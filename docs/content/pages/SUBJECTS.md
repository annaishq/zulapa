# Subjects

A subject says who acts. It stands at the start of a word and makes the
word an action: `agu` is *ear*, and `oxagu` is *I listen*. This page lists
the subjects, and what each does to the word it starts.

## The subjects

Seven subjects stand alone. A modifier after a subject says more about who
acts.

| Subject | Gloss | It says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `o` | `1` | someone, often me. It has no number |
| `i` | `2SG` | you alone |
| `a` | `3SG` | one who is not here |
| `sho` | `1PL` | us |
| `shi` | `2PL` | several of you |
| `sha` | `3PL` | several of them |
| `u` | `INF` | life itself, the infinite subject |

| Modifier | Gloss | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kei` | `MASTER` | every subject but `u` |
| `yi` | `TINY` | every subject but `u` |
| `lo` | `WET` | every subject but `u` |
| `wi`, `nu`, `to` | `NB`, `F`, `M` | `o`, `i`, `a` |
| `pi` | `SG` | `o`: `opi`, me |
| `de` | `DU` | `o`: `ode`, the two of us |
| `qa` | `SINGULAR` | `o`: `oqa`, every one |
| `pe` | `INDF` | `a`: `ape`, some of them |

A subject with its modifier acts as one subject:

    o-de-zu      ti-m
    1-DU-love    2SG.POSS-ACC
    'The two of us love you.'

## The five vowels

Five morphemes are a single vowel: `o`, `i`, `u`, `y` and `a`. Each has two
uses, told apart by its place. Before a root it is a prefix: a subject, or
an order for `y`. After a root it is a suffix: a mood.

| Vowel | As a prefix | As a suffix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `o` | `1`: `oxagu`, I listen | `PUSH`: `mao`, hold, pushing away |
| `i` | `2SG`: `ixagu`, you listen | `CARE`: `lai`, kiss gently |
| `u` | `INF`: life itself | `PULL`: towards |
| `y` | `IMP`: `yagu`, Listen! | `DANCE`: `duthay`, a dancing face |
| `a` | `3SG`: `axagu`, they listen | `LOVED`: taken to the self |

As a prefix, a vowel joins a vowel with `x`. The `y` does not: before a
vowel it is a consonant, so `y` + `agu` is `yagu`. How a mood joins a
vowel is not settled: see the [Moods](./moods.html) page.
As a suffix, `i`, `o` and `u` are adverbs, and make the word an action.
`a` and `y` are adjectives, and the word stays a noun.

## The possessives

A possessive is a consonant and a subject: `m` for the first person, `t`
for the second, and `s` for the third. It stands before a noun: `mo lapa`,
*my voice*.

| Subject | Possessive | Gloss | It says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `o` | `mo` | `1.POSS` | my, our |
| `opi` | `mipi` | `1SG.POSS` | my, of me alone |
| `i` | `ti` | `2SG.POSS` | your |
| `a` | `sa` | `3SG.POSS` | their |
| `sho` | `msho` | `1PL.POSS` | our |
| `shi` | `tshi` | `2PL.POSS` | your, of several of you |
| `sha` | `sasha` | `3PL.POSS` | their, of several of them |

`mipi` was `mopi`, the `m` and the subject `opi`. It became `mipi`: the
`i` makes it smaller, and sets it apart from `mo`. It is the small, inward
*my*, as `pi` is the small *I*.

In `sasha`, the `s` and the `sha` cannot stand together: the same
consonant does not join itself. The `a` is repeated between them.

With the accusative `m`, a possessive is the object of an action: `mo-m`,
*me*, and `ti-m`, *you*.

A possessive after `wu` says *mine*, *yours*, *theirs*: `wu-mo`, `wu-ti`,
`wu-sa`, `wu-mipi`. The noun `wu` is *emptiness*, and the verb is *to owe*.
So `wumo` is both *mine* and *what I owe*. No one owns a thing for good in
a world bound by time: what is mine is also owed.
