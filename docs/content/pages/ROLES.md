# Roles

A root of Zulapa has no class of its own. `agu` is *ear*, and it is also
*to listen*. What stands around the root gives the word its role in the
phrase: a thing, an action, or a modifier of one of them. This page lists
what gives a word its role.

## The four roles

| Role | Class | It is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a thing | noun | what a phrase is about, or what an action is done to |
| an action | verb | what happens |
| the modifier of a thing | adj | a property of a thing |
| the modifier of an action | adv | the manner of an action |

A root has a reading for each role that it can take. These are the
readings of four roots.

| Root | As a noun | As a verb | As an adjective | As an adverb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `agu` | ear | to listen | | |
| `fa` | eyes | to see | | |
| `awi` | fish | to glow | beautiful | |
| `zu` | heart | to love | lovely | lovingly |

The gloss of a root is its reading for the role of its word: `agu` is
`ear` in a noun, and `listen` in a verb.

## What gives a role

| Before the root | The word is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nothing | a noun | `agu`, ear |
| a subject | a verb | `o-x-agu`, I listen |
| `es` and a subject | a verb, in the passive | `es-o-zu`, I am loved |
| `le` | an adjective | `le-x-awi`, beautiful |
| `si` | an adverb | `si-zu`, lovingly |

A prefix sets the role. A suffix changes the meaning, and keeps the role.
